# 01 R10/R11: why the competent bot stayed at the enemy base in r02–r04, and the fix (2026-11-01)

Owner: 02-engine. Probe: `src/core/diag/bot_stall_probe.gd` (headless, private APPDATA, one run at a time under
`coordination/heavy_run.ps1`). Harness = 01 multi_seed_sim after the D30 fix: first act after U(0,3) s, then every
U(0,0.35) s with the real elapsed time; budget `low` from docs/design/balance/upgrade_budgets.json; seeds 1–5 (r05 1–10);
engine time limit 900 s. Data: balance as of f01e9d5 (text-only changes since). Raw rows: `bot_stall/` —
`bot_stall_A_diagnosis.json` (unmodified bot + cheap-first / no-reserve variants), `bot_stall_B_before_after.json`
(r01–r04 old/new + attribution, r05/r10 new), `bot_stall_C_r05_none.json` (r05 without upgrades, old/new).
`*_old` rows use `BotPlayer.use_legacy_policy()`; they reproduce the pre-fix bot exactly (r02–r04 times identical to the
first diagnosis run, made when the knobs existed but still had the old defaults).

## Cause
1. **Worker reserve trap (competent only) — the stall.** The competent bot wants 5 workers. While it has fewer, a deploy
   only had to leave **half** of the next worker's price. Melee costs 30 and is ready every 2 s, so gold went to troops
   whenever it passed `0.5 × worker_cost + 30` and almost never reached the full worker price. Result: the "competent"
   bot played r02–r04 with **2–4 workers** (r02 seeds 1/2: 2 for the whole battle; r03: 3 in every seed), always below the
   casual bot's 3 workers bought by 21–29 s, while keeping 34–57 gold unused. Its army reached the enemy base early
   (69–105 s) but was too small to finish it, and it stayed there 41–102 s while the trickle accelerated
   (2–6 enemies near the nest on average, against 0.8–1.7 for casual).
2. **Hero lock (both bots) — makes long rounds longer.** A hero below 30 % HP walked home and stayed home for the rest of
   the round: heroes do not regenerate, so "hurt" never ended. Seen in r02 competent seeds 3/5 (124–127 s at home) and r03
   casual seed 4 (254 s).
3. **Not the cause: expensive-first card order.** With the reserve trap the competent bot deployed 0–2 ranged units per
   battle, and a cheapest-first variant gave identical times in 4 of 5 r02 seeds.
Attribution (same seeds): the economy fix alone gives exactly the full-fix times in r02–r04; the hero fix alone only
shortens r02 seeds 3/5 (368 → 281 s, 371 → 291 s) and changes nothing in r03/r04.

## Fix (src/gameplay/bot_player.gd)
- Competent: save the **full** next worker price (`reserve_ratio` 1.0) unless an enemy is within 350 px of the player
  base (`threat_px`); worker target capped at the round's `workers_max` (r01 = 4; before, the bot held a reserve for a
  5th worker it could never buy).
- Both: a hurt hero holds 60 px behind the army front (inside its 80 px aura) instead of walking home.
- Casual keeps its looser economy (half reserve, 3 workers).

## What this does not show
- These are two scripted policies with timing noise, not human players. "Hurt hero holds 60 px behind the front" is one
  reasonable play pattern; a human may also keep fighting (the hero respawns at full HP) or retreat for a while.
- r06–r09 were not re-run with the new policy; r10 and r05 only at LOW (and r05 also without upgrades).
- The selftest's own no-noise bot runs (one fixed seed, no upgrades) now win every round r01–r10 (r10 at 851.6 s).
